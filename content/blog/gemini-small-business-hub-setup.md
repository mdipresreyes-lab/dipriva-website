This post covers what Google announced on October 20, 2026, the four jobs the hub gathers, the price distinction that matters before you buy, and the setup and automation routine Dipriva builds for firms with 10 to 500 employees.

## Google built one hub for small business work

On October 20, 2026, Google announced a business landing page in the Gemini app. Google described it as a central hub designed to help small business owners streamline their daily tasks. From that hub, owners can build their brand, analyze finances, understand customer insights, and more. Google said the business landing page, marketing and finance skills, and new connected apps were beginning to roll out in the coming days globally for all Gemini app users.

That rollout point matters for owners. The hub is not a separate product to evaluate in isolation. It is a landing page inside Gemini, with connections to tools a firm may already run. Google lists connections to Google Business Profile, Google Ads, and Google Workspace, plus third party connected apps such as Adobe, Canva, Dropbox, Shopify, Wix, Zoho, and Xero. A firm that already keeps its store in Shopify, its books in Xero, and its listing in Google Business Profile does not need to rebuild its business inside Gemini. It needs to connect the right pieces, in the right order, with a routine that keeps them consistent.

The risk is also plain. A landing page that can reach brand assets, finance records, and customer data can also create confusion if no one decides what connects, who approves output, and what source record wins when two records disagree. That decision work is setup. It is the work most owners skip, because the hub looks ready on day one. A tool that looks ready is not the same as a routine that is ready. The rest of this post separates the two, job by job, and then gives you the setup routine.

## Four jobs now sit in one place

The first job is brand. Google says businesses can define their brand style in Gemini by uploading their website, brand kit, fonts, or color palettes. When paired with Nano Banana 2.1, Google says Gemini can deliver end to end visual results for new product launches or ongoing marketing campaigns, and Gemini retains that visual style so owners can generate cohesive, on brand assets without repeatedly uploading style guidelines. For a roofing firm, an insurance agency, a tax practice, or a professional services firm, that means the logo, the colors, and the tone do not need to be re-explained for every post, flyer, or proposal cover. They need to be set once, checked once, and reused.

The second job is finance. Google says Gemini can turn messy spreadsheets, receipts, and purchase records into clear financial graphics and interactive models in seconds, so owners can receive financial insights in a more natural way instead of decoding numbers on their own. That speed is useful only when the graphic traces back to a source the owner trusts. A graphic that looks clear and traces to the wrong period, the wrong account, or an incomplete export is worse than a slow spreadsheet, because it looks finished. Section five below sets the check that prevents that error.

The third job is customers. The hub gathers customer insight in the same place as brand and finance, with connections to the apps where customer work already lives. That does not replace a CRM, a booking system, or a conversation with a client. It gives the owner one place to ask a clear question and check the answer against a record the firm already keeps. The value is not a new dashboard. The value is fewer copies of the same customer fact, held in fewer places, with one owner for each routine that updates it.

The fourth job is the listing. Google Business Profile is one of the connected Google tools Google names for the hub. For a local service firm, the listing is often the first page a buyer sees, and it is often the last page anyone updates. Hours change, services change, photos age, and the listing keeps the old version. The hub routine in this post treats the listing as a job with an owner and a date, not as a page that gets attention when someone complains. Brand style, finance routine, customer insight, and listing accuracy belong in one setup, because a buyer experiences them as one firm.

## Setup beats another tool every time

Most firms do not fail at AI because they pick the wrong tool. They fail because no one writes the routine. One person tries the hub, makes a few visuals, asks a few finance questions, and then returns to the old habits when the week gets busy. The hub stays open in a tab. The old spreadsheets stay open in another tab. Nothing changes, except the firm now pays for, or at least maintains, one more place to look.

Setup changes that pattern in a specific way. Setup names the apps that connect and the apps that stay out. Setup names the person who owns each routine. Setup names the source record that wins when records disagree. Setup names the approval step for financial, customer facing, and public output. Setup writes those decisions down where the team can find them, with dates for review. That written routine is what turns a landing page into operations.

## One brand style stops repeated rework

Brand work in a small firm usually fails in a quiet way. Each post, each flyer, each proposal cover starts from scratch, because no one saved the style in a place the whole team can reuse. The result is not bad design. The result is slow design, with small differences each time: a different shade, a different font, a different version of the logo, a different way of writing the service name. Buyers notice the drift even when they cannot name it, and the team pays for it in rework.

The hub addresses that failure only if the firm sets the style once and protects it. Upload the website, the brand kit, the fonts, and the color palettes. Test the saved style on one real asset, such as a service post or a proposal cover, and fix what looks wrong before the style spreads to everything else. Write down where the master brand assets live, who can change them, and what counts as an approved variation. That short document does more work than any single visual, because it stops the next person from inventing a new style by accident.

As an illustration, not a client result and not a statistic: a roofing firm saves its roof color, font, and logo once, then reuses that style for a service post, a flyer, and a proposal cover, with one person checking each asset against the saved style before it goes out. The illustration is useful because it shows the routine, not because it promises a result. The result comes from repetition, and repetition comes from a saved style with an owner.

## Finance graphics still need a human check

Finance is where speed without a check creates the largest risk. A graphic can be clear, colorful, and wrong. It can use the wrong month, mix a cash view with an accrual view, include a duplicate export, or leave out a category the owner needs to see. The owner who shares that graphic with a partner, a lender, or a team has not saved time. The owner has created a second conversation to correct the first one.

Dipriva sets a simple rule for finance output from the hub: the source record wins, every time. Before a finance graphic leaves the firm, a named person checks the period, the source file, the total, and the category against the record the firm already trusts, whether that record lives in Xero, Shopify, or a spreadsheet the owner controls. If the graphic and the record disagree, the record wins, the prompt or the source gets fixed, and the graphic does not go out. That check takes minutes, and it protects every later decision that uses the graphic.

That rule also sets a boundary for automation. Automation can prepare the graphic, organize the inputs, and draft the summary. Automation does not approve the graphic, change a financial record, or send a financial statement to a third party without a human approval step. Owners keep the final say on financial, customer facing, and public changes. That boundary is not a lack of trust in the tool. It is the control that makes the tool safe to use every week.

## Seven steps that turn the hub into routine

1. Audit connections and list only the apps the team will use each week. Disconnect or leave out the rest, so maintenance stays small.
2. Clean the business listing and brand assets before you upload anything. Fix hours, services, photos, logo files, fonts, and colors first, so the hub inherits clean inputs.
3. Save the brand style once and test it on one real asset. Fix the style before it spreads to every post and cover.
4. Connect the finance source and set the record that wins. Write down the period, the source file, and the person who checks the total before any graphic goes out.
5. Build one weekly finance routine with a human check. Prepare the graphic, check it against the source record, approve it, then share it.
6. Build one customer insight routine with a named owner. Ask one clear question each week, check the answer against a record the firm keeps, and write down the follow up.
7. Write the routine down, train the team, and review it after the first month. Correct the routine where outcomes miss, then hand it to the named owners with dates.
